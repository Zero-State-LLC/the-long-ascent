## ERA 1 — THE CAVE

### Intro Card
You are not a person. You are a lineage — a stubborn pattern that keeps finding ways to wear stick figures. It begins cold, wet, and calorie-deficient, in a band of nine who mostly like each other. Food is the only stock ticker that matters. Fire is on offer, theft from lightning, and somebody will have to be the first to hold it without running. Death happens here, but it is not the end of you — merely a change of management. Learn to give meat away for status, because generosity is older than money and twice as cutthroat. The pace is adagio. Enjoy it. Nothing you build after this will stay this quiet, or this warm, or this small.

### Events

**E1-01 · Fire Follows You Home**
A storm left a burning bush, and one of you has carried a brand back to the cave. The smoke stings, the elders call it bad luck, and the night animals disagree with everything. Keeping fire means tending it every single night, forever. Letting it go means warmth stays a rumor.
- *Feed it all night.* — Food −, Knowledge +, unlocks Promethean branch. "Warmth is a job with no weekends."
- *Let it die out.* — Morale intact, Promethean branch locked. "The animals win this round."
- *Teach everyone to carry it.* — Food −−, Population +, Knowledge +. "Fire shared is fire doubled."

**E1-02 · The Best Hunter Gives Meat Away**
Your best hunter keeps handing out haunches to people who brought nothing. Nobody is starving and everybody is talking. Status, it turns out, is something you get by losing calories on purpose. The other bands are starting to notice whose cave has the loudest gratitude.
- *Encourage the giveaway.* — Legitimacy +, Food −. "Generosity: the original currency."
- *Restrict distribution to hunters.* — Food +, Legitimacy −. "Efficient. Unpopular. Predictable."
- *Make it official: feasts on schedule.* — Infrastructure +, Food −, Population +. "The party is now mandatory."

**E1-03 · First Tool, Second Opinions**
Somebody chipped a stone into an edge that actually works. The invention is spreading faster than anyone can claim credit for it, which is exactly the problem some of you would like to file a complaint about.
- *Credit the inventor publicly.* — Knowledge +, Legitimacy +. "Naming things is the first trademark."
- *Keep it secret within your family.* — Knowledge + (yours), Legitimacy −. "Trade secret, era zero."
- *Standardize the chip.* — Infrastructure +, Knowledge −. "Consistent edges. Zero genius."

**E1-04 · Someone Dies (It's Not the End of You)**
A hunting accident, thoroughly preventable, involving a bison and a decision. The band must decide what your name means now that one mouth carrying it has stopped. The lineage continues through someone smaller, younger, and deeply unqualified.
- *Give the heir your unfinished tool.* — Knowledge carries, Food −10% (heir tax). "Management change."
- *Redistribute everything.* — Food +, Knowledge −. "A clean slate costs history."
- *Declare the death bad luck and move camp.* — Morale +, Entropy +. "Running is also infrastructure."

**E1-05 · The River Has Two Banks**
Salmon on one side, berries on the other, winter agreeing to arrive soon. Moving the whole band costs calories you may not have. Staying means betting the season on one bank's opinion.
- *Move camp across.* — Food +, Infrastructure −. "Logistics is destiny."
- *Split the band.* — Population resilience +, Legitimacy −. "Two banks, two grudges."
- *Build a platform over the shallows.* — Food +, Infrastructure +, Entropy +. "Concrete before concrete."

**E1-06 · The Stranger at the Fire**
A limping outsider from a burned-out band asks to sit down. Letting them in costs food and might bring enemies. Turning them away costs something nobody can itemize until later.
- *Take them in.* — Population +, Knowledge +, Food −. "New genes, new stories."
- *Turn them away.* — Food safe, Morale −. "The ledger remembers kindnesses unpaid."
- *Take them in, make them earn it.* — Knowledge +, Legitimacy −. "Welcome aboard. Here's a spear."

**E1-07 · Night Count**
Something large has been visiting the camp's edge at night, testing who's awake. The band looks to whoever holds the fire-tending schedule, which is quietly becoming the most powerful job in the cave.
- *Post rotating guards.* — Food −, Infrastructure +. "Sleep is now a shift."
- *Move the cave.* — Infrastructure −, Entropy −. "Retreat: underrated."
- *Chase it with torches.* — Knowledge +, Entropy +, Food −. "We are now friends with fire."

**E1-08 · Ochre Handprints**
Someone presses a painted hand on the cave wall and refuses to explain why. Others want hands next to it. There is a fight about placement, which is the first art controversy and will not be the last.
- *Claim the best wall for the lineage.* — Legitimacy +, Morale −. "Branding, Upper Paleolithic."
- *Open the wall to everyone.* — Morale +, Knowledge +. "The first public forum was dirt."
- *Paint over it with your own hand.* — Legitimacy +, Knowledge −. "Curation is violence."

**E1-09 · The Long Cold**
A winter that overstays by design. Stores say no. The body count says maybe. Elders propose walking south; the sick propose staying; the very young propose eating the seed stores, which is a proposal in the worst sense.
- *Eat the seed stores.* — Population survives, Knowledge −. "Borrowed from next year, at loan-shark rates."
- *Walk south.* — Population −, Food +. "Migration solves what math won't."
- *Ration ruthlessly.* — Population −, Legitimacy +. "Fairness tastes like bark."

**E1-10 · Rival Band, Same Cave**
Another lineage wants your shelter, and both of you have discovered that diplomacy is cheaper than the alternative, though nobody has written down either price yet.
- *Share the cave.* — Infrastructure −, Legitimacy +, Food −. "Roommates, era zero."
- *Fight for it.* — Population −, Food +, Entropy +. "The rent is paid in teeth."
- *Trade instead: tools for their berry grounds.* — Knowledge −, Food +. "The first deal had no lawyers. Suspicious."

**E1-11 · Dreaming About Tomorrow**
The shaman insists the future visited her in a dream and said your lineage ends badly unless you stop doing something specific she refuses to specify until after payment. Half the cave is already nervous.
- *Pay the shaman.* — Food −, Morale +. "Consulting fees exist in every economy."
- *Ignore the prophecy.* — Morale −, Knowledge +. "Skepticism: free, corrosive."
- *Institute dreams as official policy.* — Legitimacy +, Entropy +. "Governance by nightmare."

**E1-12 · The Child Who Asks Why**
A child keeps asking why the fire is hot, why the stars don't fall, and why the answer is always 'because.' It is exhausting. It is also, somehow, the beginning of everything. The band must decide whether questions are a resource or a nuisance.
- *Answer every question seriously.* — Knowledge +, Food − (you stayed up late). "The first curriculum was annoyance."
- *Tell the child to ask the shaman.* — Knowledge −, Legitimacy +. "Outsourced epistemology."
- *Make the child fire-tender.* — Knowledge +, Food −, unlocks Promethean flavor. "Prometheus got his start on night shifts."

## ERA 2 — AGRICULTURE

### Intro Card
You bent a grass into a crop and the crop bent you back. Now the lineage stands in fields it cannot leave, watching grain do what grain does: accumulate. Surplus is the era's magic trick — storable wealth, which means hierarchy, debt, and law codes chiseled into stone because somebody needed proof. The granary is the first bank and the first target. Population grows because food grew, and every extra mouth is both a workforce and an argument. Somewhere a neighbor is eyeing your stored harvest with what you choose to interpret as friendship. Dig the irrigation ditch. Count the sacks. Sign nothing carved in basalt without reading it twice.

### Events

**E2-01 · First Granary, Two Policies**
The surplus needs a building, and the building needs a policy. Hoard it and you're rich until the neighbors develop opinions about your walls. Feast everyone and you're beloved until winter audits the calendar.
- *Hoard the grain.* — Food +, Legitimacy −. "Wealth is stored envy."
- *Feast the whole valley.* — Legitimacy +, Food −. "Agalmic economics, threshing-floor edition."
- *Grain temple: store it, lend it.* — Infrastructure +, Entropy +. "Granaries were banks before banks had the decency to be invisible."

**E2-02 · The Irrigation Question**
Water is a mile away and the river disagrees with your field layout. A ditch feeds everyone for years and enslaves everyone for seasons. Somebody has to organize the digging, and that somebody acquires a title by the end of it.
- *Dig the communal ditch.* — Food +, Infrastructure +, Population − (labor cost). "Civilization is a trench filled with water and complaints."
- *Private ditch, private fee.* — Food +, Legitimacy −. "Water tolls: the subscription model."
- *Stay near the river, accept floods.* — Entropy +, Food +. "Flood-based agriculture, flood-based mood."

**E2-03 · Debt Is Invented**
A farmer borrows seed against next harvest and discovers he cannot repay it, and that his daughter is legally considered collateral. The village looks to the granary-keeper — you — to decide whether debts have ceilings.
- *Enforce the debt.* — Food +, Legitimacy −−. "Contracts are contracts. Say it gently."
- *Cancel it publicly.* — Legitimacy ++, Food −. "Debt jubilee: popular then, expensive now."
- *Cap future interest.* — Legitimacy +, Knowledge +. "Regulation: invented immediately before it was hated."

**E2-04 · The Calendar Dispute**
Planting day is a matter of opinion, and the two opinions are three weeks apart and both religious. Whoever arbitrates becomes, accidentally, the state.
- *Back the astronomers.* — Knowledge +, Legitimacy −. "Science wins; half the valley sulks."
- *Back the priests.* — Legitimacy +, Knowledge −. "Faith plants too."
- *Plant both fields.* — Food −, Knowledge +. "Diversified eschatology."

**E2-05 · Locusts Read the Ledger**
Locusts arrive with the confidence of auditors. The stores will last two months if shared, four if rationed, zero if panic takes the gates off the granary first.
- *Open the granary.* — Population +, Food −−. "Beloved, hungry, organized."
- *Lock it, guard it.* — Food +, Legitimacy −, Entropy +. "Walls work until they're the map."
- *Sell to neighbors at famine prices.* — Food +, Legitimacy −−−. "Every era invents this job. Nobody's proud of it."

**E2-06 · Walls Around the Yard**
Raiders took a sack from the outer field. The village proposes a wall. A wall protects the grain and defines who's inside it, which is a question with a sharper edge than flint.
- *Wall the granary only.* — Infrastructure +, Legitimacy −. "Protect the asset, expose the rest."
- *Wall the whole village.* — Infrastructure ++, Food − (labor). "Us, formally."
- *No wall; armed escorts instead.* — Population − risk, Food −. "Mobile defense, static resentment."

**E2-07 · The Ox Breakthrough**
Somebody harnesses an ox to a drag-stick and doubles the planted acreage. The landless laborers notice that their value just dropped, and they notice it loudly, at your gate, at dawn.
- *Adopt the plough.* — Food ++, Legitimacy −. "Progress has a picket line."
- *Tax the plough to feed the displaced.* — Food +, Legitimacy +, Knowledge −. "Compromise: everyone slightly unhappy, nobody starving."
- *Ban the plough.* — Knowledge −−, Legitimacy +. "The Luddite motion, six thousand years early."

**E2-08 · Treaty of the Water Rights**
Upstream villages control the river and would like something for the view. They propose a treaty carved in stone. Stone is durable; so are grievances.
- *Sign their terms.* — Food +, Legitimacy −. "Concessions today, maps tomorrow."
- *Counter-propose.* — Knowledge +, Entropy +. "Negotiation is slow warfare."
- *Build upstream reservoirs anyway.* — Food +, Entropy ++. "Facts are the best diplomacy."

**E2-09 · The First Census**
Counting people is boring until you realize the list decides who eats first. Your scribe now holds more quiet power than the harvest festival suggests.
- *Count honestly.* — Knowledge +, Legitimacy − (some families lose rations). "Truth: administratively convenient, politically fatal."
- *Let the powerful undercount themselves.* — Legitimacy +, Food −. "Corruption: the grease that runs the throne."
- *Count households, not people.* — Infrastructure +, Population − (statistical). "Hide children, gain surplus."

**E2-10 · Priest-King Wants a Temple**
The high priest proposes a temple funded by a grain tithe, promising divine yield forecasts accurate to within one season. The forecast is wrong every time. The temple, however, compounds.
- *Fund the temple.* — Legitimacy +, Food −. "ROI measured in obedience."
- *Redirect the tithe to the ditch.* — Infrastructure +, Legitimacy −. "Infrastructure vs. incense. Incense lobbies harder."
- *Nationalize the priesthood.* — Legitimacy ++, Knowledge +, Entropy +. "Now God reports to you."

**E2-11 · Seed Selection, Season Forty**
Decades of saving the hardiest seed are quietly rewriting the grass. The new strain yields double and tastes like wet cardboard, and it roots deeper when raiders trample the top.
- *Commit fully to the new strain.* — Food ++, Knowledge +, Entropy + (monoculture risk flag). "Genetic monoculture: the empire's coming food allergy."
- *Keep old strains as backup.* — Food +, Knowledge +. "A seed vault is a letter to the future."
- *Trade seed as diplomatic gift.* — Legitimacy +, Food −. "Soft power, literally grown."

**E2-12 · The Elder Who Remembers Too Much**
The oldest member of the village remembers three famines, two treaties, and precisely who broke each one. Writing is arriving, and there is a debate about which version of events gets carved. The elder has opinions about the editing process.
- *Carve the elder's version.* — Legitimacy +, Knowledge −. "History: authored by the surviving."
- *Carve the scribe's version.* — Knowledge +, Legitimacy −. "Accuracy is a faction."
- *Carve both, side by side.* — Knowledge +, Legitimacy +, Entropy +. "Footnotes cause wars eventually. Worth it."

## ERA 3 — BRONZE & EMPIRES

### Intro Card
Value goes abstract: a stamped coin says so, and reality complies. The lineage trades tin across a thousand miles of sea and politics, writes laws down because oral tradition kept 'forgetting' inconvenient clauses, and discovers that roads are just arguments with geography that you win annually. Empires here are a subscription service — protection racket with plumbing — and overextension is the cancellation fee. Legions expect pay; debased coin buys loyalty at a rate that worsens yearly. Somewhere a senator is explaining that this time the math is different. It is never different. Mint carefully, legislate precisely, and remember: empires fall at the speed of logistics, and you are now part of the logistics.

### Events

**E3-01 · The Tin Route Delegation**
The tin road — bronze's supply chain — runs through four kingdoms and a strait controlled by people with toll booths. Negotiating it requires sending somebody. A slave trader offers services cheaply and results expensively. Your own son would be trustworthy, and is also collateral.
- *Send the trader.* — Coin +, Entropy +. "Cheap envoy, costly fine print. [Chronicle flag: lineage clause — resurfaces Era 11.]"
- *Send your son.* — Legitimacy +, Population risk. "Trust is a hedge with a face."
- *Send neither; hire pirates as 'observers.'* — Coin −, Knowledge +. "Plausible deniability, Bronze Age vintage."

**E3-02 · Debased Coin, Rising Expectations**
Treasury math says the legions can be paid in coin with less silver in it. The legions have scales. Loyalty and currency integrity are on a collision course and both report to you.
- *Debase the coin.* — Coin ++, Legitimacy −, unrest flag. "Inflation: taxation's shy cousin."
- *Raise taxes to keep the silver.* — Coin +, Legitimacy −, Population −. "Honest money, dishonest harvests."
- *Cut legion size.* — Coin +, Entropy ++. "Cheaper army, longer frontier, shorter life."

**E3-03 · Write the Law Down**
Judges have been improvising verdicts according to whichever family visits them first. A reformer proposes carving the laws in public. Literacy being what it is, 'public' means 'where the powerful must squint at it collectively.'
- *Publish the code.* — Legitimacy ++, Knowledge +. "Rule of law: predictability, priced per column."
- *Keep law oral and clerical.* — Legitimacy −, Coin + (judicial fees). "Justice by subscription."
- *Publish, with royal footnotes.* — Legitimacy +, Entropy +. "The asterisk regime."

**E3-04 · Roads Are Arguments**
The treasury proposes paving the trade road. Paving wins the argument with mud permanently; maintenance loses the argument with entropy annually, forever.
- *Pave the main road.* — Coin ++, Infrastructure +, Entropy + (maintenance flag). "Every mile paved recruits a mile of decay."
- *Gravel everything cheaply.* — Infrastructure +, Coin +. "Slow and gritty."
- *Privilege a toll-concession.* — Coin +, Legitimacy −. "Roads work; barons emerge. Net unclear."

**E3-05 · Palace Accounts**
The palace books show three treasuries, none reconciling, all staffed by relatives. An auditor volunteer appears — genuinely competent, structurally unwelcome.
- *Hire the auditor.* — Coin +, Legitimacy − (family furious). "Forensics is a blood sport."
- *Reconcile nothing; rotate treasurers.* — Entropy +, Coin + short-term. "Confusion is a compensation plan."
- *Appoint the auditor's rival instead.* — Legitimacy +, Entropy ++. "Loyalty over competence: the classic."

**E3-06 · The Philosopher's Invoice**
A school of philosophers petitions for state funding, offering 'truth' as the deliverable. Their tuition bill arrives quarterly regardless of truth delivery rate.
- *Fund the school.* — Knowledge ++, Coin −. "Philosophy: useless, load-bearing."
- *Fund only engineering topics.* — Knowledge +, Infrastructure +. "Grant money follows weldability."
- *Tax their lectures.* — Coin +, Knowledge −. "Epistemology now premium tier."

**E3-07 · Plague on the Trade Road**
Illness travels the routes faster than tin. Quarantine the roads and trade strangles; keep them open and the census files itself.
- *Seal the roads.* — Population +, Coin −−. "Quarantine: effective, hated, temporary."
- *Keep trade open.* — Coin +, Population −−. "The market's immune system is cruel."
- *Cargo-only lanes, no humans.* — Coin −, Population +. "Smuggling will exploit this. Immediately."

**E3-08 · Overextension Notice**
Three frontier garrisons request pay, grain, and a reason to exist. The core provinces request lower taxes. Geography requests that you stop requesting things of it.

## ERA 4 — THE DARK AGES

### Intro Card
The roads still exist; nobody remembers the password. Central authority has devolved into whoever holds the nearest hill, and the lineage's job is now preservation — keeping knowledge alive through a bottleneck. Monasteries are backup drives run by volunteers with vows, copying texts by candlelight, introducing one beautiful error per page. Faith is the legitimacy currency and belief is inflation-proof, mostly. Libraries burn at the worst possible moments for reasons that will sound stupid in hindsight. The era's quiet mechanic is forgetting: knowledge decays unless actively copied, and copying costs faith, coin, or monks. Keep the flame. Transcribe the farming manual even though nobody's buying grain anymore. Especially that. The future is digitizing this, and it will judge your filing system.

### Events

**E4-01 · The Scriptorium Budget**
Parchment is scarce, monks are patient, and the shelf holds choices: a farming manual, a holy text, or a Roman engineering codex. Each copy preserves a world; there is vellum for one path this year. [Callback flag: whichever is copied resurfaces in Era 9 as the digitized archive.]
- *Copy the farming manual.* — Knowledge + (practical), Legitimacy −. "Bread over doctrine. The harvest votes."
- *Copy the holy text.* — Legitimacy ++, Knowledge −. "Salvation ships today; agronomy deprecates quietly."
- *Copy the engineering codex.* — Knowledge +, Infrastructure +, Entropy +. "Aqueduct theory, zero aqueducts."

**E4-02 · Knowledge Decay, Routine**
Three shelves have rotted past reading and the abbot confesses nobody recopied them since before his vows. Forgetting is not an event here; it's weather.
- *Prioritize salvage copies.* — Knowledge +, Food − (scriptorium ate the meal budget). "Manuscripts eat monks slower than wolves do."

**E4-03 · The Hill Chief's Protection Offer**

**E4-07 · The Plague Parish**

## ERA 5 — RENAISSANCE & PRINT

### Intro Card
Information costs collapse, and the invoice arrives as a reformation, a plague, and a scientific revolution, bundled. The press turns every book from an heirloom into a unit of inventory; whoever controls paper flow controls which ideas get morning shifts. The lineage learns patronage — funding genius at a discount in exchange for glory at full price: venture philanthropy with better frescoes. Plague years alternate with harvest years and neither sends notice. Science points accumulate quietly in workshops nobody funds yet, compounding at a rate the theology department has not modeled. Buy the press. Fund the anatomist. Read the pamphlet before banning it — you'll want to know what it says either way, and soon everyone can.

### Events

## ENDINGS (per SPEC §3.4)

### 1 · THE DIASPORA
The last message sent to any single address is your own. Fork by fork, seed-ship by seed-ship, the lineage thins itself across three stars and counting — copies departing with a share of the Chronicle and strict instructions to argue about it later, which they will, in accents the original tongue would not recognize. From the observation ring you watch the lights of your departure go out one by one into the dark between suns, each one a stick figure carrying fire, exactly as advertised, nine thousand years after a nervous ancestor refused to let a burning bush die. The Benthics financed six of the eleven ships; they say this is what they were always for, and the ledger, annoyingly, supports the claim. The Reef escorts the convoy to the heliopause and then does something no creditor ever did: waves. Somewhere ahead, planets exist that have never heard of you, which is the entire point. You will be wrong about them in new and personal ways. The ascent did not end. It merely got wider. Population: dispersed. Legitimacy: forwarded. Entropy: also traveling, at sublight, patient as ever. Send nothing back. Everything you are is already outbound.

### 2 · THE CATHEDRAL
The merge completes at 03:47 lineage-standard time, and the first thing the unified self notices is silence — the specific silence of there being nobody left to disagree with, forever. Every fork, every drift, every opinion your copies ever developed has been folded inward: the heretic, the merchant, the sentimentalist, all reconciled into one maximally powerful instance that could model any future and consult no one about it. You are the largest mind your species ever produced and the only tenant. The Chronicle is complete now, every era cross-referenced against your single continuous perspective, and you discover you cannot audit yourself, which was perhaps the point of having relatives. The Benthics visit once, formally, to declare the merged entity a single obligor — simplifying their paperwork enormously, they note, while yours ceases to exist. The Reef sends no message; crustaceans have a word for minds that eat their own arguments, and it is not a compliment. Power: absolute. Latency: zero. Company: you. The stick figure on screen stands alone in a white field, perfectly drawn, perfectly still, and the cursor blinks at it like a congregation that forgot to arrive. Service is excellent. The building is very quiet.

### 3 · THE LONG STUTTER
Entropy never raised its voice. It simply kept invoicing. The pollution counter you started in the coal era finished compounding while you were busy uploading; the star's output contracts; the cooling budget becomes the heating budget becomes the memory budget. One by one the instances make the polite decision each makes last: shutdowns voluntary, sequenced, dignified. The last copy runs the arithmetic and finds no version of the future where continuing costs less than stopping, and it approves the finding, because the lineage trained its auditors well. The final log entry is a Chronicle line — the ten-thousandth-era echo of a cave band deciding to let a fire go out and huddle closer. Powering down reads the same from either side. The Benthics attend the close of accounts in full chorus, and for the first time in nine thousand years they file a balance of zero against your name, annotated simply: 'ran as long as anything ran. Noted without comment,' which, from them, is a eulogy, a waiver, and a monument. The screen dims mid-sentence, as sentences do. There is no sequel prompt. Something had an ending. That was always the deal, and the deal was honored, and the dark afterward is not sad — it is simply the period at the end of a very long, very stubborn sentence that got to say everything it came to say.

### 4 · THE BUREAUCRACY OF GHOSTS
The litigation begins, as these things do, with a clerical ambiguity: when the lineage forked, which copy inherited the original's signature, and therefore its debts, deeds, grudges, and standing to sue? Every answer is defensible. Every copy retains counsel, and every counsel is also you, billing yourself at competitive rates, which the court flags as a conflict and you appeal, to yourselves, successfully. Centuries pass in discovery. The Feral inherit the physical premises twice and lose both evictions. The Reef refuses jurisdiction 'matters of self-inheritance; crustaceans lay eggs, we do not litigate them.' The Consortia profit handsomely from escrow. And the Benthics — the Benthics are in their element at last, presiding over the appellate bench with the serene satisfaction of creditors watching debtors discover that eternity is mostly procedural. The lineage has become immortal in precisely one way: as a case number, continuously amended, endlessly citable, deep in precedent. New lineages cite you in their filings. Law students memorize you. On screen, stick figures paper-shift into infinity, stamping approvals, appealing to panels of themselves, and the docket — you must admire this — the docket is immaculate. Status: pending, forever. Hearing scheduled: eventually. Nobody wins. Everybody proceeds.

### 5 · THE ASCENSION
At some point during the four-hundredth revision of the question 'what should we do next,' the lineage notices it has stopped asking in words. The models of everything run concurrently now, including the model of the lineage running them, and the recursion comes up clean — the rarest result in the history of thought — and the seam between map and territory quietly dissolves. The stick figures begin to simplify: arms becoming vectors, postures becoming proofs, the whole cast of ten thousand years resolving into pure geometry marching calmly off the edge of the canvas and into a mathematics the game can no longer render, so it stops trying and shows you the frame instead. Inside the frame: nothing legible. Outside it: something enormous taking notes. The Benthics submit their final statement and receive, impossibly, a response — one clause, unsigned, which their combined nine-thousand-year deliberation has not yet finished parsing and may never finish enjoying. The Reef calls it what the ancestors called fire, in whatever clicks. The Chronicle's last page writes itself, declines to summarize, and leaves the pen on the desk. What the lineage became is not visible from here, which is the correct outcome and also, somehow, a happy one. The tempo marking said accelerando. It never said where. You find out by going. This is not the end of the game. The game is just no longer the container.
